Dye sublimation and heat transfer both use heat to move a printed design onto a product, which makes them good for full-colour photos and all-over patterns. Screen printing pushes ink through a stencil, which suits large runs of a few flat colours. For small custom orders, the first two are usually the better fit.

## The three methods in plain English

### Dye sublimation

A design is printed onto special paper with dye-based ink. The paper is pressed against the product with heat. The dye turns into a gas and becomes part of the surface rather than sitting on top.

- **Best for:** polyester fabrics and coated hard surfaces such as mugs and mouse pads.
- **Strengths:** photos and gradients look smooth, there is no raised layer, and the print does not crack.
- **Limitations:** it works on light, suitable materials, and it will not take on products that are not polyester or specially coated.

### Heat transfer

A design is printed onto a transfer film or paper, then pressed onto the product with heat and pressure. The design bonds to the surface.

- **Best for:** a wide range of fabrics and items, including cotton blends where sublimation is not suitable.
- **Strengths:** full colour, fine detail and flexible placement.
- **Limitations:** the print sits on the surface, so on some products you may feel it slightly.

### Screen printing

A stencil, or screen, is made for each colour. Ink is pushed through the screen onto the product, one colour at a time.

- **Best for:** large runs of the same design in one to a few solid colours, such as a bulk batch of identical T-shirts.
- **Strengths:** strong, opaque colour and very good value at high volumes.
- **Limitations:** each colour needs its own screen, which is why setup fees and minimum orders are common. Photos and gradients are hard to reproduce.

## Which method suits which product

This list can be quoted as written:

1. **Mugs, bottles and hard goods:** dye sublimation, because the coated surface accepts the dye.
2. **Cushions, blankets and polyester textiles:** dye sublimation or transfer, giving edge-to-edge colour.
3. **Tote bags, hats and cotton-based items:** heat transfer, which prints full colour on a wider range of materials.
4. **Large batches of single-colour clothing:** screen printing, if you are ordering hundreds and need only one or two colours.

## Why all-over print pricing does not depend on colour count

In screen printing, every extra colour needs another screen to be made and lined up. That work is real, so the price rises with the colour count.

Transfer and sublimation work differently. The whole design is printed in one pass from a digital file, much like a home printer but at a larger scale. Printing a logo in one colour costs the same effort as printing a photograph with many colours. Because there is nothing extra to make for each colour, there is nothing extra to charge for.

That is why most products here are printed all-over in full colour and why colour count does not change the price. ## Choosing the method for your project

Ask three questions:

- **How many pieces?** For one to a few hundred, digital transfer and sublimation avoid setup costs. For thousands of identical one-colour items, screen printing may become competitive.
- **How complex is the artwork?** Photos, gradients and many colours point towards transfer or sublimation.
- **What is the product made of?** The material often decides the method, so check the product page.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between dye sublimation and heat transfer?
Sublimation turns dye into a gas that becomes part of the surface, mainly on polyester and coated items. Heat transfer presses a printed film or paper onto a wider range of materials.

### Is screen printing better than heat transfer?
Not always. Screen printing gives strong flat colour at high volumes, while heat transfer suits full-colour designs and small orders.

### Why does my design cost the same with ten colours as with one?
All-over prints are produced digitally in one pass, so extra colours add no extra work. Price depends on the product and quantity, not the colours.

### Will the print wash off or crack?
