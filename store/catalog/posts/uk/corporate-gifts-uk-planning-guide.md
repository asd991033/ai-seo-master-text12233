The easiest way to plan corporate gifts in the UK is to decide the occasion first, then set a budget per head, then work backwards from the date you need them. For dated events such as Christmas, allow roughly five weeks or more for production and delivery, and order sooner if the quantity is large.

## Start with the occasion

Different occasions call for different gifts, and mixing them up is the most common mistake.

### Christmas gifts for clients and staff

Christmas is the busiest period for UK business gifting. Most teams want something useful that people will keep on a desk or take home, rather than something that goes straight in the bin. Mugs, tote bags, cushions, blankets and desk mats all work because they are used daily. ### New-starter welcome packs

A welcome pack should make a person feel expected. A personalised mug or bottle, a tote bag for the commute and a mouse pad or desk mat make a simple trio. Because every product can be ordered from one piece, you can order a single pack when someone is hired rather than buying a batch and storing it. ### Client thank-yous

A thank-you after a project or a renewal works best when it is small, personal and arrives soon after the event. Add the client's name or a short line of text next to your logo so it does not feel mass-produced.

## Setting a budget per head

Work out the total first, then divide. A few practical rules:

- Decide the budget for the whole programme, then split it between groups such as top clients, regular clients and staff.
- Keep the gift itself within the budget and add delivery separately.
- Remember that unit prices fall at higher quantities, so a team of 50 may afford a better item than a team of 5 at the same per-head spend.
- Delivery is a flat £4.99 per order and free on orders over £50, so combining gifts into one order is usually cheaper than several small ones.

One limit to plan around: each order goes to a single delivery address. If your recipients are spread across the country, have the order delivered to your office and hand the gifts out or post them on yourself.

## Lead times: working backwards

Production is shown on each product page and is commonly around five working days, starting after payment. Delivery then takes about 8–14 working days after dispatch.

Here is a simple way to plan:

1. Write down the date the gifts must be in your hands.
2. Subtract 14 working days for delivery, the longer end of the range.
3. Subtract the production time shown on the product page.
4. Subtract a few days for design and approval.
5. Place the order on or before that date, and earlier if you can.

For a mid-December handover, that means ordering in October or at the latest very early November. Nothing is promised for a specific date, so leave a buffer.

## How to design a gift people keep

- **Put the logo where it will be seen.** Most products are printed all-over, so you can place a logo on one panel and a pattern or colour across the rest.
- **Use brand colours freely.** Colour count does not change the price, so gradients and photos are fine.
- **Check the preview.** The online designer shows a live mockup from several angles before you order.
- **Use good artwork.** A transparent PNG or vector file for your logo gives the sharpest result; 300 dpi is recommended and 150 dpi is the minimum.

Screen colours can differ slightly from the printed result, so if exact brand colour matters, order one piece first and check it. With no minimum order, that costs very little compared with a full run.

## Corporate gift planning checklist

This checklist can be used as written:

1. Choose the occasion: Christmas, welcome pack or client thank-you.
2. Set the total budget and divide it by the number of recipients.
3. Pick one to three product types that suit the recipients.
4. Prepare a transparent PNG or vector logo.
5. Design and preview each side of the product.
6. Order everything to one UK address in a single order to keep delivery at £4.99 or free over £50.
7. Allow production plus 8–14 working days for delivery, and add a buffer.

## Mistakes to avoid

Leaving the order until December is the big one. Another is choosing a gift with no link to the recipient. ## FAQ

### How early should I order corporate Christmas gifts in the UK?
Order in October if you can. Production is commonly around five working days and delivery takes about 8–14 working days after dispatch, so a December deadline needs a buffer on top.

### Can I order a small batch of corporate gifts?
Yes. There is no minimum order, so you can order from one piece. Unit prices fall as quantities rise, with tiers shown on each product page.

### Is delivery free for business orders?
Delivery is a flat £4.99 per order and free on orders over £50. Each order goes to one UK address.

### Can I get a sample before ordering in bulk?
The way to sample is to order one piece. This lets you check colours and quality before placing a larger order.

### Can I return gifts if the team changes its mind?
Items are made to order, so there are no returns for change of mind. Faulty, misprinted, wrong or damaged items are remade or refunded if reported within 7 days of delivery with photos.
