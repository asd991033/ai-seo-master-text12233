## Mug, tumbler or bottle: a quick comparison

Each drinkware type has a natural home. Think about where it will be used, not just how it looks.

### Custom mugs

Mugs are the classic choice for hot drinks at a desk, in a break room or at home. They are easy to gift, they sit visibly on a desk, and the print area is a simple wrap around the body. Good for: coffee and tea drinkers, office gifts, fan merch.

### Custom tumblers

A tumbler is made for carrying. It suits commuters, drivers and anyone who walks between meetings. Good for: team gifts, creator merch and welcome kits.

### Custom water bottles

Bottles are best for hydration on the move: gyms, hiking, school and travel. They tend to be used all day, so your design gets seen often. Good for: sports clubs, event giveaways and wellness programs.

## How to decide

Ask three questions.

1. **Where will it be used?** Desk and kitchen point to mugs. Commutes point to tumblers. Active days point to bottles.
2. **Who is it for?** A gift for a coworker may suit a mug. A gift for a runner suits a bottle.
3. **What will the design say?** A wide wrap with an illustration works on most drinkware, while a small logo may look better on a slimmer bottle.

Check each product page for the real size, capacity and care details, since those vary by product.

## Designing wrap-around artwork

A curved surface changes how artwork looks. A flat design on screen will bend around the body, so plan for it.

### Think in panels

Imagine the drinkware turning in your hand. The front is what people see first, the back is seen when it is picked up, and a seam or handle may interrupt the design.

- Put your main logo or message in the center of the front view.
- Keep key text away from edges and from the handle area.
- Use a repeating pattern or a continuous illustration if you want an unbroken wrap.

### Use the live mockup

The online designer shows a live mockup from several angles. Rotate it fully before you order, and look for text that wraps out of sight or a logo that sits too close to an edge.

### Prepare clean files

- Use a **transparent PNG** or **vector** file for logos.
- Aim for **300 dpi**, with 150 dpi as a minimum.
- Extend background color or pattern past the area you care about, so no blank gaps show.
- Keep important details inside a safe area, away from the edges.
