To prepare artwork for all-over printing, use images at 300 dpi (150 dpi minimum), upload logos as transparent PNG or vector files, extend backgrounds past the edge, and keep important text inside a safe area. Expect printed colors to differ slightly from your screen.

## What all-over printing means for your file

Most products in the store are printed all-over in full color, by transfer or dye-sublimation. The design can cover the entire printable surface, and photos and gradients are fine. The number of colors in your artwork does not change the price.

That freedom comes with one responsibility: your file has to be good enough to cover a large area. A small or blurry image becomes obvious when it is stretched across a blanket, a tote or a cushion.

## Resolution: the first thing to check

Resolution is how much detail your image contains for the size it will be printed.

- **300 dpi** is recommended.
- **150 dpi** is the minimum.
- Below that, edges look soft and photos look pixelated.

### Practical tips

- Start with the largest original file you have. Do not upscale a small image to make it bigger, because it will not add real detail.
- Avoid screenshots and images saved from social media. They are often compressed.
- Look at the warning in the online designer. It tells you when an image is too low in resolution for the size you chose.

If you cannot get a better file, make the image smaller within the design instead of stretching it.

## Transparent PNG vs vector

Both formats are good for logos, for different reasons.

### Transparent PNG

A PNG with a transparent background lets the product color or your background show around your logo, with no white box. Export it at a large size, ideally 300 dpi at the printed dimensions.

### Vector files

A vector file stores shapes and lines as math rather than pixels, so it can be scaled up or down without losing sharpness. It is the ideal format for a logo that may appear on a mug one day and a blanket the next.

### Which should you use?

- **Logo or icon:** vector if you have it, otherwise a high-resolution transparent PNG.
- **Photo or painting:** a high-resolution image at 300 dpi.
- **Text-only design:** vector, or add the text directly in the designer.

Avoid JPG files for logos. They have no transparency, so a white box will usually appear around your artwork.

## Bleed and safe area

Printed products are cut, sewn or shaped after printing, and small shifts can happen. Two concepts protect your design.

### Bleed

Bleed means extending your background color, pattern or image slightly beyond the area where the design ends. If the edge moves a little, no blank strip appears.

### Safe area

The safe area is the inner zone where important elements should stay: faces, logos, text and key details. Anything too close to the edge could be trimmed, folded or hidden in a seam.

- Keep text well inside the edge.
- Do not place a logo where it may wrap around a corner.
- On curved items such as drinkware, keep key details away from the handle zone.

Use the live mockup to rotate the product and check each side. Each print side can be designed separately.

## Quotable checklist: artwork prep in six steps

**How to prepare artwork for all-over printing:**

1. Start with the largest, cleanest original file.
2. Save at 300 dpi (never below 150 dpi).
3. Use a transparent PNG or vector for logos.
4. Extend backgrounds past the edge for bleed.
5. Keep text and key details inside the safe area.
6. Check every angle in the live mockup before ordering.

## Color expectations and ordering

Screens glow, and prints reflect light, so colors never match perfectly. Expect small differences.

- Very bright, neon-looking colors may print a little softer.
- Dark shades may look slightly different from a bright monitor.
- Fabric and ceramic surfaces can change how a color appears.

## FAQ

### What resolution do I need for all-over printing?

Use 300 dpi if you can. The minimum is 150 dpi, and the designer warns you when an image is too low.

### Should I upload a PNG or a vector file?

For logos, a vector file is ideal and a transparent PNG is also great. Both avoid a white box around your artwork.

### What is bleed and do I need it?

Bleed is background artwork extended slightly past the edge, so small shifts do not leave blank strips. It is a good habit for any full-coverage design.

### Will printed colors match my screen?

Not exactly. Screen colors can differ slightly from the printed result. Order 1 piece first if an exact shade matters.

### Can I design the front and back differently?

Yes. Each print side can be designed separately, and the live mockup shows the product from several angles.
