Conference and trade show merch gets kept when it is useful in daily life, looks good enough to be seen in public, and carries a small, tasteful logo. Bags, drinkware and tech accessories are the safest picks because people use them at work, at the gym and on the commute, which keeps your brand in front of them long after the event.

## What makes merch worth keeping

Delegates leave a big expo with a bag full of items, and most of it is thrown out within a week. The items that survive tend to pass three tests:

- **Useful at least weekly:** a drink bottle, a tote, a mouse pad.
- **Looks like something they would buy:** clean design, not a billboard.
- **Easy to carry home:** flat, light and not fragile, especially for interstate and international visitors flying out.

Ask yourself one question before ordering: where will this item physically live in someone's life, and how often will they see it?

## How to design merch people will not bin

### Keep branding small and confident

A neat logo and a simple URL usually beat a full panel of slogans. Printing is full-colour, so you can use a bold pattern or illustration as the main design and leave your logo as a quiet signature.

### Use the print areas properly

Each side can be designed separately. Put an eye-catching design on one side and your details on the other. Check the live mockup from every angle in the designer before you order.

### Make the artwork file print-ready

- Logos as a transparent PNG or vector file.
- 300 dpi recommended, 150 dpi minimum; the designer warns if resolution is too low.
- Expect slight colour differences between screen and print.

## Quantity and budget planning

You do not need a huge pallet. There is no minimum order, and unit price falls as quantity rises, with tiers shown on each product page (for example 50+, 100+, 300+).

A practical way to decide quantity:

1. Estimate delegates plus staff plus a small buffer for speakers and sponsors.
2. Decide whether everyone gets one item, or only visitors to your stand.
3. Split between two item types if you want a premium option for qualified leads.
4. Check whether your total passes A$200 for free delivery. Otherwise delivery is a flat A$20 per order.

Handing out the item only on request or after a conversation at the stand reduces waste and makes recipients value it more.

## Timeline: working back from your event date

Here is the production and delivery sequence in plain steps:

1. Finalise artwork and pay by card through Stripe checkout.
2. Production begins after payment, commonly around 5 working days.
3. Dispatch, then about 8 to 14 working days to reach an Australian address.

That adds up to roughly four weeks, so plan to order at least five to six weeks before bump-in. For a large launch, order one piece first to check the print, then place the full order. Delivery is to a single address, so have it sent to your office or the venue contact rather than to individual staff.

## Making merch part of the conversation

- Mention the item when you invite people to your stand.
- Add a short, genuine line of text (a tagline or event name and year) that dates the item without making it feel like clutter.
- Use the same design on your team's own bags so your stand looks coordinated.

## FAQ

### What is the best merch to give away at a trade show?
Items people use daily, such as tote bags, drink bottles and mouse pads, are the safest choices. They are practical, easy to carry and keep your logo visible after the event.

### How many items should I order for an expo?
Estimate delegates plus staff plus a small buffer, then decide whether to give one to everyone or only to stand visitors. There is no minimum, and bigger quantities lower the unit price.

### How early should I order for a conference in Australia?
Order at least five to six weeks before the event. Production commonly takes around 5 working days after payment, then delivery takes about 8 to 14 working days after dispatch.

### Can I order a single sample first?
Yes. Ordering one piece is the way to see the real product before a bigger run, since physical pre-production samples are otherwise not offered.

### Can the merch include embroidery or engraving?
No. Products are printed, mostly full-colour by transfer or dye-sublimation, so embroidery and engraving are not available on these items.
